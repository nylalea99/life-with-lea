When I'm sad, I cook. Not because I'm trying to fix the sadness, but because there's something about the act of cooking that helps me process it.

## The Ritual of It

Chopping vegetables requires focus. Measuring ingredients requires precision. Stirring a pot requires patience. When my mind is racing with anxious thoughts, these small, focused acts bring me back to the present moment.

## Comfort Food, Literally

There's a reason we call certain foods "comfort food." It's not just about taste — it's about the memories and feelings attached to them.

My go-to when I'm feeling low? Simple pasta with garlic, olive oil, and parmesan. It's what my grandmother used to make me when I was a child, and every bite brings me back to her kitchen.

## It's Not About the Food

Sometimes the finished dish isn't even the point. The point is that I did something with my hands. I created something. In moments when everything feels out of control, cooking gives me a small corner of the world that I can manage.

## If You're Going Through Something

I'm not a therapist, and cooking isn't a replacement for professional help. But if you're looking for a small, grounding activity during a tough time, I encourage you to try it. Pick something simple. Focus on the process, not the outcome.

You might be surprised at how much better you feel when you sit down to eat something you made with your own hands.
